TRADING PSYCHOLOGY:

Coming Back Stronger — Drawdown Management

The neuroscience of loss: A severe loss triggers a "mental smoke alarm" (cortisol rush) that impairs prefrontal-cortex logic for 4–6 hours. Loss aversion makes losses hurt twice as much as equal gains.

Infinite vs. finite game: Trading is an infinite game of capital protection. A 50% loss needs a 100% gain just to break even.

5-step re-entry system: Hard circuit breakers, 25% micro-sizing, 20-trade process audits, pre-written rules and gradual scaling.

MARKET STRUCTURE:

The Great 3:15 PM Plot Twist

The 3:15 PM disconnect: SEBI's Closing Auction Session (CAS) stops cash trading at 3:15 PM while F&O runs to 3:40 PM, creating execution price gaps.

Retail trader revolt: The #NoTradeDay boycott, fuelled by CAS, a 150% STT hike on futures, fewer weekly expiries, bigger lots and 50% cash-margin rules.

Ecosystem shift: The end of high-frequency retail scalping and a move toward algorithmic and longer-term, cash-backed positions.

The Domino Effect

Monetary tightening: RBI's 25 bps hike to 5.50%, driven by $100+ crude, sticky inflation (5.2% forecast), weather risks and a weaker rupee.

Sectoral impact: Banks gain on wider NIMs; real estate and auto slow on costlier loans; IT acts as an export hedge.

Investor reality: Costlier margin funding, rising 10-year yields and the end of cheap-liquidity speculation.

Resurrecting a Stock Exchange

01 · MACROECONOMIC POLICY

RBI Tightens Rates Amid Global Oil & Inflation Pressures

India's central bank ended nearly four years of steady borrowing costs by raising the repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50%. Governor Sanjay Malhotra confirmed a shift to "calibrated tightening", taking future rate cuts off the table to curb accelerating domestic price pressures.

Key drivers behind the policy shift

•  Surging energy costs: Middle East conflict pushed crude above $100/barrel, raising transport and manufacturing costs.

•  Sticky inflation: CPI moved toward 5.0%, lifting the full-year forecast to 5.2%.

•  Currency depreciation: A weaker rupee raised the cost of imported raw materials.

Sectoral & asset class impact matrix

Sector / Asset Immediate Reaction Implications for Traders
Commercial Banking Floating-rate loans reprice faster than deposits. Short-term NIM expansion for large private lenders like HDFC and ICICI Bank.
Real Estate & Auto Higher financing costs for home and auto loans. Cooling demand in housing and vehicle sales as EMIs rise.
IT & Exporters Little domestic debt; revenue earned in foreign currency. Rupee weakness cushions margins, making IT a relative safe haven.
Commodities & Debt 10-year yields spiked; oil stays geopolitically driven. MTF costs rise; valuations must rest on real earnings, not cheap liquidity.

02 · EXCHANGE INFRASTRUCTURE

The Historic Revival of the Calcutta Stock Exchange

The 118-year-old CSE is making national headlines after its unlisted share price doubled within 90 days — from ~₹900 in June 2026 to ₹2,100–₹2,175 by September 2026. Following the state finance minister's budget address, the CSE board asked SEBI to put its voluntary exit application on hold, signalling an ambitious institutional comeback.

Dispelling the myths

•  Solid balance sheet: Though platform trading has been suspended since April 2013, CSE's net worth exceeds ₹300 crore. It earned ₹253 crore from a land sale on Kolkata's EM Bypass and ₹26 crore in FY25 from interest and listing fees.

•  Addressing the "missing middle": NSE/BSE listing fees leave smaller regional MSMEs behind. A revitalised CSE offers a low-cost incubation platform for Eastern India's businesses.

•  Active infrastructure & legacy scale: 1,507 listed companies, ~500 brokers and institutional shareholders including BSE (5.05%) and WBIDFC (3.4%).
