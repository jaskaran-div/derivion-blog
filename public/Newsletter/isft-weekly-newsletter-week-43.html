01 · GEOPOLITICS VS. EARNINGS

Embassy Evacuations & Middle East Escalation Drive Market Volatility

Global markets faced intense intraday volatility as a Wednesday rally in benchmark equity indices proved short-lived. A sudden wave of risk-off sentiment took hold on Thursday morning after reports that the U.S. State Department had issued ordered departures for non-emergency U.S. government employees and family members in Saudi Arabia.

The threat landscape remains high amid reports of renewed US-Israel strike plans on Iran after the US midterms and IRGC threats regarding advanced weaponry - offsetting the earnings optimism generated by SpaceX results and tech gains.

02 · CENTRAL BANKS & ASIA

Global Central Bank Divergence & Regional Asian Overview

Central banks are navigating starkly contrasting inflation dynamics, balancing structural commodity price spikes against domestic growth momentum and foreign exchange risks.

Japan (BoJ): 83% of surveyed analysts expect a hike at the December 18 meeting (up from 35% last month), while 100% expect a hold on October 30. USD/JPY remains near 158.10 as the Nikkei dropped 0.5%.

Australia (RBA): Deputy Governor Hauser called the landscape a "central bankers' nightmare", noting businesses now view 3.0–3.5% as a structural inflation floor, with surging cocoa prices (>$5,600/tonne) presenting persistent price risks.

New Zealand (RBNZ): Q3 CPI printed lower than expected at +3.7% y/y (vs. the RBNZ's 3.9% forecast) on lower fuel prices. Westpac maintains a 25bp hike forecast to 3.00% for October 28.

China & South Korea: The PBOC injected CNY 48bn via reverse repos, but the CSI 300 (-1.2%) and Hang Seng (-1.8%) fell. The Bank of Korea held its repo rate at 3.00% after two consecutive hikes, upgrading growth projections on chip export demand.

ASEAN tightening: The Philippines' BSP raised rates 25bp to 5.25% (4th straight hike) on El Niño food inflation; Indonesia's BI held at 5.75% while cutting reserve requirements.
